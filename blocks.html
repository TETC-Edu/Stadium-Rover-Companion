What are blocks?

Never seen any of this before? Read this first. It takes about two minutes, and then Level 1 will make a lot more sense. Nothing here is a test.

A block is a command

A block is one instruction for the robot. You don't type it — you drag it from a list and drop it into your program. This one tells the rover to drive forward 450 millimeters:

drive forward 450 mm

The white bubble is a number you choose. Click it, type a different number, and the rover drives a different distance. That's it — that's the whole idea.

Blocks snap together

One block on its own doesn't do much. You snap them together into a stack, like LEGO. The robot runs the stack from the top down, one block at a time, in order:

when started
turn right 30 degrees
drive forward 450 mm
kick medium

Read that out loud: turn right 30 degrees, drive forward 450 millimeters, kick. That's a delivery. You just read a program.

The rounded one on top is a hat block — when started. Every stack begins with one. It's what tells the robot "this is where to start when someone presses play."

Order is everything

The robot does exactly what you stacked, in exactly that order. It won't guess what you meant. If the kick block sits above the drive block, the rover kicks the cargo before it goes anywhere — and the cargo ends up in the wrong place.

That's not the robot being broken. That's the robot doing precisely what you told it. Most of the work in this lesson is figuring out the difference.

The blocks you'll use

drive forward 450 mm
turn left 90 degrees
kick medium
place
wait 0.5 seconds

Five blocks. Kick launches the soccer ball. Place sets a barrel down gently. Wait pauses so things settle. That's the whole toolbox for Level 1.

Blocks are real code

Here's the part people don't expect: blocks aren't a toy version of coding. Each block is a line of code wearing a costume. In Level 2 you'll flip a switch and see the exact same program written as Python text:

Your blocks

turn right 30 degrees
drive forward 450 mm
kick medium

The same thing in Python

robot.turn_for(RIGHT, 30)
robot.move_for(450, 0)
robot.kicker.kick(MEDIUM)
So you're already writing Python. You just haven't typed it yet. In Level 2 you'll flip your blocks over and read the Python underneath — and by Level 3 you'll be typing it yourself.
